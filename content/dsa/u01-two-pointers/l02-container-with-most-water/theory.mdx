<Hook>
"Given wall heights, pick two walls that hold the most water." Brute force over all pairs is the obvious answer. The
interviewer wants the O(n) version, and then asks the question that matters: **why is it safe to throw a wall away?**
If you can say that in one sentence, you pass.
</Hook>

## The picture: two people holding a tarp

Two people hold a tarp between them to carry water. The water can only rise as high as the **lower** person's hands,
because it spills over the low side. And the further apart they stand, the more the tarp holds. So:

**area = (the shorter wall) × (the distance between the walls)**

Start them at the two ends, the widest the tarp can ever be. Now one of them has to step in. Which one?

**The shorter one.** If the taller one steps in, the tarp is still capped by the same short person, and it just got
narrower. That can never help. Moving the short one is the only move that might find a taller partner.

</Explain>

## Why throwing the shorter wall away is safe

This is the answer to say out loud. Suppose the **left** wall is the shorter one. Every container we have not measured
yet that still uses this left wall pairs it with some wall `r'` **to the left of** `right`. Such a container is:

- **narrower**, because `r'` is closer;
- **no taller**, because the level is at most the left wall, which is the short side anyway.

Narrower and no taller means smaller or equal. So no unmeasured container using this left wall can beat the one we
just measured. The left wall is finished: `left++`. The same argument works for the right wall, and with a tie
either wall can go.

Every step removes one wall for good, so there are at most n steps: **O(n) time, O(1) space**.

</Explain>

## Run it: the code, piece by piece

**1. Start widest:** `left = 0`, `right = length - 1`, `best = 0`.

**2. Measure:** the level is `Math.min(height[left], height[right])`, the width is `right - left`.

**3. Record before moving:** `best = Math.max(best, area)`, so the container you just measured is never lost.

**4. Discard the shorter wall:** `if (height[left] < height[right]) left++; else right--;`. On a tie, `else` moves
`right`, which the argument above allows.

The real trace of the first steps:

<Callout kind="java">
`h * (right - left)` is an `int` multiplication. With LeetCode's limits (100,000 walls up to 10,000 tall) the
biggest area is about 10⁹, which still fits in an `int` (max about 2.1 × 10⁹). With bigger limits you would need
`long`. Saying this unprompted is a cheap senior signal.
</Callout>

## The trap: "just move one pointer every time"

People who memorised "two pointers, move inward" without the reason often write `left++` every step. The demo has
that wrong version too:

```output
always moving left on [5, 1, 1, 1, 1, 9, 4] -> 24 (correct 25)
```

The best pair is walls 0 and 5 (heights 5 and 9: level 5, width 5, area 25). Always moving left drops the 5 on
the very first step, while the right wall is still the short 4. After that no container can reach 25.

And the referee line, comparing with brute force over every pair:

```output
random check: 5000 / 5000 match brute force
```

</Explain>
<Panel>

| Approach | Time | Space | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| All pairs | O(n²) | O(1) | easy to get right: the referee |
| **Two pointers, move the shorter** | **O(n)** | O(1) | needs the exchange argument |
| Two pointers, move either | O(n) | O(1) | **wrong**: can discard the best wall |

<Callout kind="interview">
"Start with the widest pair. The area is capped by the shorter wall, so every narrower container using that wall is
no better. That wall is done, so I move it inward. Each step discards one wall: O(n) time, O(1) space."
</Callout>

## Break it (predict first, then run)

1. Swap the comparison so the **taller** wall moves. What does the example return now?
2. Move `best = Math.max(best, area)` to *after* the pointer move. Which container is lost?
3. Change `while (left < right)` to `while (left <= right)`. What area does the last iteration add, and does the answer change?
4. Make the heights `long` and multiply two of the biggest values. When would an `int` overflow here?

<CheatCard title="Cheat card: Container With Most Water">

- area = min(h[left], h[right]) × (right - left). Start at both ends, the widest container.
- Record `best` first, then move the **shorter** wall inward (tie: either).
- Why safe: every unmeasured container using the shorter wall is narrower and no taller.
- O(n) time, O(1) space. Brute force over pairs is O(n²).
- Only the two chosen walls matter; walls in between do not block anything. (That changes in Trapping Rain Water.)

</CheatCard>
